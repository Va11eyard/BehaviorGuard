% 2. Linguistic component (s_ling) ablation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Linguistic component ablation.}
On the corrected PersonaChat holdout ($N{=}10{,}165$ test messages, 29 positives),
the default composite with $s_{\text{ling}}$ active at fixed $\tau{=}0.60$
severely degrades detection (Table~\ref{tab:personachat-ablation}, row~1:
F1 $=0.004$ [0.000, 0.009], recall $=0.069$, 2/1078/27).
$s_{\text{ling}}$ excluded (\texttt{linguistic\_component\_enabled=False})---renormalizing
weights to semantic/temporal only---recovers ranking and recall at the same
fixed threshold (row~2: fixed-$\tau$ ablation F1 $=0.314$ [0.143, 0.474],
precision $=0.364$ (not F1), recall $=0.276$, 8/14/21).
Because the single-split validation half contained only 11 positives
($<12$), headline threshold selection for the cosine baseline uses 5-fold
user-level CV (row~3): $\tau^*{=}0.61$ uniformly across all five folds,
yielding pooled F1 $=0.364$ [0.170, 0.536] (headline metric; 8/7/21) at
unchanged recall $=0.276$ but improved precision $=0.533$.
Relative to the $s_{\text{ling}}$-included configuration, the improvement
proceeds in two steps: F1 $0.004 \rightarrow 0.314$ (fixed-$\tau$ ablation;
$s_{\text{ling}}$ removal at $\tau{=}0.60$) $\rightarrow 0.364$ (CV-validated
$\tau^*$; pooled F1), not a direct jump from 0.004 to 0.364.
Relative to row~2, CV $\tau^*$ selection raises F1 from the fixed-$\tau$
ablation value $0.314$ to pooled F1 $0.364$ without changing recall on this
partition (precision improves from $0.364$ (row~2 precision) to $0.533$).
We treat $s_{\text{ling}}$ exclusion as a confirmed measurement-and-fix result;
production defaults remain unchanged pending manuscript review.

% 3. Mahalanobis semantic scoring

\paragraph{Mahalanobis semantic scoring.}
Replacing cosine $s_{\text{sem}}$ with a per-user Mahalanobis distance
($s_{\text{ling}}$ excluded) does \emph{not} improve pooled F1 under the
same 5-fold CV protocol (Table~\ref{tab:personachat-ablation}, row~4:
$\tau^*{=}0.36$, F1 $=0.273$ [0.095, 0.444], 6/9/23, vs.\ row~3 cosine
pooled F1 $=0.364$ [0.170, 0.536], 8/7/21).
Mahalanobis improves ROC-AUC directionally on the pooled CV test scores
($0.936$ vs.\ $0.917$, $\Delta{=}+0.019$; bootstrap 95\% CI on the AUC
difference includes zero, $[-0.062, +0.087]$, $n{=}5000$, seed~42), but
does not dominate the precision--recall curve at deployable operating points:
at precision $\geq 0.5$, cosine achieves recall $=0.276$ at $\tau^*{=}0.61$
whereas Mahalanobis reaches only recall $=0.069$ at its F1-optimal
$\tau^*{=}0.36$.
Figure~\ref{fig:cv-semantic-pr} reconciles the two views: Mahalanobis's
ranking advantage concentrates in the low-FPR ROC region (mean $\Delta$TPR
$+0.282$ at FPR $\leq 0.05$) without translating to better precision--recall
performance at the F1-optimal $\tau^*$ thresholds marked on the main panel.

\paragraph{Recall gap, statistical power, and scope (deferred to v2).}
Our corrected PersonaChat evaluation injects synthetic adversarial turns into
35 of 5{,}000 user timelines (0.7\% user coverage), yielding 126 injected
messages in the full dataset and 29 positive test messages under the standard
80/20 per-user holdout.
These results support \emph{proof-of-concept} detection across three template
families (account takeover, social engineering, prompt injection) but do
\emph{not} establish statistical significance or broad generalization to
arbitrary anomalous behavior; headline metrics are point estimates with 95\%
CIs (bootstrap for F1; exact Clopper--Pearson for recall/precision where
reported).

\emph{Representational limitation (Pattern~B).}
Among the 29 held-out positives at fixed $\tau{=}0.60$ with $s_{\text{ling}}$
excluded (8 detected, 21 missed), missed injections occupy a non-overlapping
lower $s_{\text{sem}}$ region relative to detected positives (detected:
$M{=}0.659$, $SD{=}0.056$, $n{=}8$; missed: $M{=}0.469$, $SD{=}0.025$,
$n{=}21$; pooled $SD{=}0.036$; Cohen's $d{=}5.31$, canonical $n$-weighted
pooled SD; Mann--Whitney $p{=}4.6{\times}10^{-5}$).
Zero of 21 missed positives have $s_{\text{sem}}$ above the minimum detected
value ($0.608$).
Although 95\% of missed positives have composite scores within $0.08$ of
$\tau{=}0.60$, a composite $\tau$-sweep on the same holdout scores does not
support a viable threshold trade-off: fixed-$\tau$ ablation F1 falls from
$0.314$ at $\tau{=}0.60$ (full holdout, row~2) to $0.033$ at $\tau{=}0.55$
(15/870/14).
Per-type recall (ATO 6/26; SE 1/2; PI 1/1) is exploratory only for SE/PI
($n{<}15$ each).

\emph{Statistical power.}
With $n{=}29$ test positives and CV-validated baseline pooled F1 $=0.364$,
verified power to detect F1 improvements is well below commonly cited targets
(two-proportion recall test at fixed $P{=}0.533$: ${\sim}15\%$ / ${\sim}39\%$ /
${\sim}68\%$ for $\Delta$F1 $+0.05/+0.10/+0.15$; simulation-consistent
${\sim}23\%$ / ${\sim}50\%$ / ${\sim}77\%$), versus asserted 40\% / 60\% / 80\%.
Wide CIs (e.g., cosine pooled F1 [0.170, 0.536]) reflect this constraint.

Expanding injection coverage and embedding capacity is explicit
post-submission work (BehaviorGuard v2).
